# Vision — why this course exists

**Thesis in one line:** Frontier LLMs are *far* more powerful than this audience
thinks, and once you can drive them from plain text in a terminal, that power is
yours for research and teaching — with your judgement still in the loop.

## The misconception we are here to break

Most faculty picture an LLM as fancy autocorrect: a spellchecker, an email
drafter, a way to smooth over clunky prose. That undersells what these systems
do by *orders of magnitude*.

The honest, current headline: frontier models are, right now, doing genuine
mathematics and near-superhuman competitive programming. They are **not** merely
for correcting your prose or the other cheap tasks people quietly hand them.

If a participant walks out still thinking "it's a prose tool," we have failed —
no matter how comfortable they got with the terminal.

## The two goals

**(a) POWER — awareness of what these things can actually do.**
Aim high. Show the ceiling: proving/attacking hard math, competitive
programming, real research work end to end. The intro (~45 min) is about power
on purpose — see `intro-notes/current-capabilities.md` for the raw material.

**(b) TOOLS + honest CONSTRAINTS — the kit to actually use them.**
The practical, unglamorous half that turns awe into ability:

- **Plain text over Word** — diffable, durable, and the format models handle
  best. (Everything in this repo is Markdown / Quarto / BibTeX for this reason.)
- **Basic git** — the safety net. Experiment on copies, revert fearlessly.
  "Start with copies" is cheap insurance, not bureaucracy.
- **Skills** — reusable, checkable workflows (e.g. a citation-checker that
  verifies every reference exists and is cited correctly).
- **Terminal comfort** — the whole point is *not being scared of it*. No prior
  shell knowledge assumed.
- **Speech-to-text** — lowers the typing barrier; part of the workflow we teach.

## The pedagogical bet: lead with POWER

We open with awe, deliberately. A professor who has just watched a model solve
problems they *know* are genuinely hard will forgive the terminal's friction and
lean into learning the tools. Inspiration first, plumbing second.

## What we are NOT saying

Not hype for its own sake, and not "AI does everything." We show the ceiling
**and** the honest floor:

- **They do not do 100% of your work — and the part they can't do is the part you
  already own.** What stays human is the *ideas and the judgement*: deciding what
  is worth doing, and checking it was done right. The best current evidence puts
  the human–AI research gap mostly in *ideation*, not execution — the model is a
  superb executor of a good idea, not a reliable source of one. For this audience
  that is reassuring, not threatening: our colleagues are not short of ideas. The
  AI removes the *execution* friction between their idea and a finished result.
- **You stay responsible.** The model supplies speed and execution; you supply the
  idea and the judgement. Verification (simulations, citation checks, actually
  reading the output) is part of the craft, not an optional extra.

Power **and** humility. Both are true at once, and saying so is what makes the
power credible to a skeptical, smart audience.

## What success looks like (per participant)

- Leaves genuinely impressed **and** genuinely capable — has run an agent on
  their *own* real material at least once.
- Understands *why* plain text, git, and skills matter — not just that we said so.
- Is no longer scared of the terminal.
- Is correctly calibrated: knows the power is real, and knows exactly where they
  must still supply judgement.
