## What to change

1. **Git needs its own mandatory deck, right after setup.** People struggled to
   see how git differs from Dropbox or OneDrive, and I wasn't well prepared for
   that question. Frame it as mainly for the agents, but very good for you too.
   Establish its pedigree (Linus Torvalds, used by nearly every software
   developer), so it doesn't look like a quirk of mine. Cover the cloud too:
   GitHub, and the words agents use (commit, revert, push, pull, remote).
2. **The agent opening the PDFs upset people.** In the cleanup exercise (the
   WISEflow export), the agent reads the submissions to rename them, and the
   room got worked up about it. Either a different exercise, or make privacy the
   planned point of it. Undecided.
3. **People were far more worried about access rights than I expected,** and I
   wasn't prepared. It needs proper slides: my own stance (give it lots of
   access), how to restrict it, the auto-classifiers, and the permissions file.
4. **The level difference in the room was huge:** about 15 derailments over the
   day, some of them substantial.
5. **At least one participant never got Claude running** and sat through four hours
   without it.
6. **The afternoon menu ("you choose") didn't work:** nobody had an opinion. People
   can't choose between research workflows they haven't seen yet. Better: let them
   choose among words they've heard (skills, connectors, subagents…), which needs
   no prior knowledge, only curiosity. (Added 21 October.)
