## Today’s finish line

Build one teaching artifact you can plausibly use this autumn: an exercise,
rubric, assessment set, course page, slide deck, or small package containing
several of these.

## Morning — the course factory

Begin with a syllabus or a short description of one course. Tell the agent who
the students are, what they should learn, and what evidence would convince you
that they learned it.

Then ask for a small system rather than a pile of generic content:

1. draft assessment questions at named difficulty levels;
2. build a grading rubric from the learning outcomes;
3. generate a strong, mediocre, and subtly wrong answer;
4. grade those answers using the rubric;
5. critique the questions and revise the weakest ones.

You are not outsourcing the decision about what students should learn. You are
using cheap drafts to examine that decision more closely.

## Afternoon — publish, then react

Turn rough notes into something visible. The agent may create a webpage, a slide
deck, or both. The first version should arrive quickly; most of the learning is
in what happens next.

Try instructions such as:

> This is too text-heavy. Keep one argument per screen and move the explanation
> into speaker notes.

> Add a ten-minute pair exercise after the central example. Make the
> instructions understandable without me in the room.

> Make a printable one-page version for students and check that it agrees with
> the slides.

> Build the project, inspect any errors, and repair them.

Notice the difference between generating text and maintaining a collection of
related artifacts.

## A working bullshit-detection routine

Before shipping:

- **Scope:** Did the agent use only material it was allowed to use?
- **Evidence:** Can important factual claims be traced?
- **Consistency:** Do slides, handouts, rubrics, and answers agree?
- **Audience:** Is the assumed reader actually your student?
- **Inspection:** Have you opened the rendered result?
- **Responsibility:** Would you put your name on it?

## Take-home artifact

Use the final block to finish one thing, not begin five things. Ask the agent to
write a short `README.md` explaining what exists and how to update it later.

Commit a checkpoint with git. The artifact does not have to be perfect. It has
to be real, inspectable, and yours.
