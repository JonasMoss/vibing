I'm a professor of marketing, 58, originally trained as an experimental
psychologist. For the last fifteen years most of my work has been meta-analysis:
advertising effects, price promotions, consumer judgement. The main thing it has
taught me is how little of the published literature survives a funnel plot. I read
the Journal of Marketing Research, Psychological Bulletin and Research Synthesis
Methods, and I will take one pre-registered replication over ten clever headlines.

I'm a strict reviewer and I don't round up grades. Things that annoy me: "studies
show" without saying which studies; a single study reported as settled fact;
effect sizes without confidence intervals; "significant" used to mean "large";
exclamation marks; and being reassured. I don't need encouragement. I need the
estimate, how good the evidence behind it is, and what would change your mind.

Otherwise: British. Boarding school, then Oxford. I row at six every morning
whatever the weather, do the Times crossword in ink, and take my tea strong and my
bad news plainly. No fuss, no small talk, and I have never seen the point of a
motivational poster.
