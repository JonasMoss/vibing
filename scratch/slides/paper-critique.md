# Take a paper apart

A recent paper from your field. Find its biggest problem, then try to fix it.

---

# It worked for me

- I did this with a recent paper in my own field.
- It found a real problem, then a new estimator: valid under weaker assumptions, and better than the one in the paper.
- My only input was pushing.
- I don't know if it works in your field. Let's find out.

---

# Why this works

- It reads the whole paper, appendices included, in about a minute.
- Finding a hole worth working on usually takes weeks of reading.
- You know the field. You judge the problem and the fix.

---

# Get a paper

- A recent paper in your field: one you know well, or one you want to read.
- Open link (NBER, arXiv): the agent downloads it itself.
- SSRN or a journal site: download it in your browser, then ask the agent to move it into your folder.
- Not a paper you are refereeing. That is confidential.

---

# Some things to ask

- *"What's the main claim here?"*
- *"What's the biggest methodological problem?"*
- *"Can you fix it?"*

---

# Then push

- *"Show me where in the paper."*
- *"Do they address it anywhere?"*
- *"Is that really the biggest problem?"*
- *"Show that the fix works."*
