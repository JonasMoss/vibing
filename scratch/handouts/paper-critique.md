# Take a paper apart

*Research session handout. Pick a recent paper from your own field, let the agent
read all of it, find its biggest problem, and then ask it to fix it. Finding a hole
worth working on usually takes weeks of reading. Here it takes a minute, and you
spend your time judging instead.*

> **The floor: one problem you have checked against the paper yourself.** A fix
> that holds up is the bonus. Whether the agent finds something sharp or something
> silly, you will know more about how far to trust it in your own field.

---

## 1. Get a paper

Pick a **recent paper in your field**: one you know well (so you can judge the
answers), or one you have been meaning to read.

- **If it has an open PDF link** (NBER, arXiv and similar), just give the link:

  > Download this paper into this folder: *paste the link*

- **If it is on SSRN or a journal website**, the agent is usually blocked. Download
  the PDF in your browser as usual, then ask:

  > Move the PDF I just downloaded into this folder.

- **Never use a paper you are refereeing.** It is confidential, and it should not
  go to an AI service.

If the agent says it cannot read the PDF, tell it to find a way. It may ask to
install a small tool; say yes.

---

## 2. Some things to ask

These are suggestions, not a script. Talk to it like a colleague who has just read
the paper.

- *What's the main claim here?* Check this against your own reading. If it gets the
  claim wrong, everything after it is suspect.
- *What's the biggest methodological problem?*
- *Can you fix it?*

A theory paper, a case study or a qualitative paper works too: the problem may be
an assumption, the case selection or the evidence behind the main result.

---

## 3. Then push

The first answer is rarely the best one. Some pushes that work:

- *Show me where in the paper.* Every claim should come with a quote. Find the quote
  in the paper yourself; it takes seconds.
- *Do they address it anywhere, including the appendix?* A first critique is often
  already answered somewhere in the paper.
- *Is that really the biggest problem?*
- *That's generic. What's specific to this paper?*
- *Show that the fix works.* It may write and run a simulation, or fetch public data.

A fix usually takes **5–10 minutes**: it writes and runs code, often a simulation.
Let it work. If it asks to install Python, say yes.

---

## 4. If you have time

- Try a second paper, or one you know is flawed, and see whether it finds the flaw.
- Try it on your own working paper, before a referee does.

---

## For the round-up

Write down:

- the best problem it found, with its quote;
- the best fix, and whether you believe it;
- the worst false alarm: something it claimed that the paper already handles.

**Keep the folder.** The next block, "Leave a trail", turns it into something a
co-author could rerun.
